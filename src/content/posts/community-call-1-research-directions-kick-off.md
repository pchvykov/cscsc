#### **Mapping a New Research Frontier: Core Questions in Complex Systems & Contemplative Studies** 

This document synthesizes discussions from a nascent interdisciplinary community exploring the intersection of complex systems science and contemplative studies. Our goal is to identify promising, concrete research directions that bridge first-person experience with third-person formal modeling, with an eye toward foundational questions and potential funding opportunities (e.g., Templeton Foundation).

The ideas are organized into three interconnected thematic areas: **Meta-Methodology**, **Telos and Wholes**, and **State Induction & Optimization**.

---

#### **1. Meta-Methodology: Foundations for a New Interdisciplinary Science**

Before tackling specific questions, we must establish how we will work together. This section addresses the foundational practices and assumptions of our community.

- **Shared Axioms and the Ineffable:** What shared axioms can we take for granted when studying subjective experience (the "ineffable") with formal tools? How do we maintain meta-awareness of our own experience and of the process of formalization itself?
- **Interdisciplinary Communication:** How can we communicate effectively across vastly different fields (e.g., anthropology, neuroscience, mathematics, philosophy)? We can learn from fields like anthropology and history/philosophy of science that have long grappled with these challenges.
- **Practices to Overcome Implicit Assumptions:** What individual or collective practices can help us avoid being trapped by our implicit assumptions?
  - **Proposals:** Adopting practices like "scientific circling" (deep, non-judgmental dialogue) or creating a modern "glass beads game" to find connections across disciplines.
  - Establishing new norms, such as treating citations as a gratitude practice rather than merely a metric.
- **Suspension of Disbelief:** How can we cultivate a "suspension of disbelief" to allow contemplative practices to genuinely take our research in new directions?
- **Concrete Piloting:** A core proposal is to **select a single, concrete question** and collaboratively construct the practices and methods needed to address it. For example, a causality reading group could start with the direct experience of free will and end with formal models of causality.

---

#### **2. Telos: Explaining Parts from Wholes, Roles, and Meaning**

This theme focuses on a fundamental shift in perspective: understanding components of a complex system not from the bottom-up, but in terms of their role, purpose, or meaning within a larger whole that we intuitively understand.

- **Core Challenge: Formalizing "Wholes":** Mainstream fields like anatomy, sociology, and linguistics already explain parts (organs, individuals, words) by their function within a whole (body, society, language). The challenge is to **mathematically formalize concepts like "role," "purpose," and "meaning."**
- **Abstract Analogies for Roles:** Can we develop abstract, formal analogies for what it means to "play a role" in a larger system? This requires moving beyond purely bottom-up approaches (e.g., emergent phenomena from simple legos) and finding ways to formally represent the context-dependent identity of a part.
- **Multiscale Representations:** Building on work in complex systems (e.g., Ochoa, 2025), how does the environmental context at multiple scales define what an object or process *is*?
- **Embodied Understanding through Art and Myth:** How can we use art, cinema, and myth to provide an embodied, intuitive experience of these abstract truths? For instance, a story like the "golden egg laying goose" can be a way of knowing that is complementary to formal analysis.

---

#### **3. State Induction & Optimization: Bridging Theory, Experience, and Intervention**

This theme addresses the practical challenge of understanding, inducing, and optimizing specific states of consciousness (e.g., meditative states) for both scientific inquiry and public health benefit.

- **The Central Gap: Between Brain and Experience:** A significant gap exists between advanced complex systems methods for analyzing brain data and the rich landscape of psychological states (levels of "selfing," identification, reification, focused attention). **How do we rigorously bridge brain states with subjective experience?**
- **The Measurement Problem:** How can we measure the process of meditation or contemplation without interfering with it? We need non-intrusive proxy measures for internal psychological processes.
- **Induction and Acceleration of States:**
  - **Core Question:** Why are contemplative practices difficult, and can this process be accelerated or induced by other means? Is this analogous to physical exercise, or fundamentally different?
  - **Intervention Modalities:** Exploring a spectrum of induction methods, including:
    - **Technological:** Precision Direct Current Stimulation (PDCs), Transcranial Focused Ultrasound (TFUS), Targeted Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (TMS), Augmented/Virtual Reality.
    - **Pharmacological & Digital:** "Numadelics" or "technodelics," digital therapeutics, hypnosis, mHealth apps.
  - **Ethical & Efficacy Questions:** If states can be induced rapidly (e.g., via a pill or device), is the result as "nourishing" as traditionally cultivated states, or is it "fast food"? What can be achieved without sustained effort?
- **A Control-Theoretic Approach:** Can we apply control theory from dynamic systems to the brain? The open problem is to collect the right data and design experimental paradigms to build models that allow us to optimally nudge a brain system into a desired state (e.g., a specific meditative state) using precise interventions. This would be a large-scale, collaborative effort with profound basic science and clinical implications.

---

#### **Synthesized Core Directions for Grant Proposals**

1. **A Pilot Project on a Single Question:** Fund a focused, interdisciplinary working group to take one question (e.g., "the experience of free will") and develop the novel methodologies required to study it, from first-person practices to formal modeling.
2. **The Mathematics of Telos:** Support research to develop a formal, mathematical framework for understanding "roles," "purposes," and "context-dependent identity" in complex systems, potentially using multiscale information theory.
3. **The State Induction Bridge:** Fund a large-scale project to collect high-density, multi-modal data (brain, physiology, subjective report) during contemplative practices and various induction methods. The goal is to build a control-theoretic model of state transitions to bridge the gap between brain dynamics and subjective experience, with direct applications to mental health and well-being.
