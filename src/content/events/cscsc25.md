### About the Conference

Our inaugural workshop explored the intersection of complexity science and contemplative studies, bringing together researchers from diverse backgrounds to collectively define the core questions and opportunities of this emerging field. The conference embodied both contemplative practice and scientific rigor through a balance of keynote talks, discussion sessions, mindfulness practices, and collaborative workshops.

#### Vision & Goals

Acknowledging the long history and challenges of attempts to formalize contemplative philosophy as a hard science, we proposed that the young field of complexity science provides new opportunities for this age-old quest. Our three-fold intention:

1. **Foster community** among experts working on either side of this intersection
2. **Collectively define** the core questions and opportunities of this integration
3. **Embody the ideals** of contemplation and scientific rigor we are bridging

[Learn more about our vision](https://luma.com/3vyrtz4j)

### Keynote Speakers

#### Michael Levin (Tufts University)

**"Minds in the Platonic Space"**

Exploring the Platonic space and a modern version of minds that are not "in" the physical space, including controversial ideas around the claim that there are no "dumb machines" or "inanimate matter." Working to understand how to recognize, build, and ethically relate to embodied minds in the physical world.

#### Martin Nowak (Harvard University)

**"The Beyond Within"**

Discussion of his recent contemplative books "Beyond" and "Within," which explore science, mathematics, and philosophy as ways to understanding. These books discuss how to lead life and describe the eternity that is before us—or actually within us.

#### Gary Lupyan (University of Wisconsin–Madison)

**"How Language Helps Structure the Human Mind"**

Exploring whether language merely reflects objective categories of our mental world, or if they are (in part) the creations of language itself, and examining language's key role in structuring the human mind and potentially being a necessary ingredient for the emergence of human-like intelligence.

### [Conference Schedule](/gatherings/cscsc25/schedule/)

The three-day conference followed a dialectical progression:

**Day 1: Foundation and Vision Setting** - "Thesis"<br>Setting the stage: "What are we building together?"

**Day 2: Methods Alignment** - "Antithesis"<br>Embracing the challenge: "How do we focus our inquiry?"

**Day 3: Looking Ahead** - "Synthesis"<br>Co-creating: "How do we sustain and grow this field?"

Each day included:

- Contemplative opening practices
- Keynote presentations
- Mindfulness practices from diverse traditions
- Parallel breakout discussion sessions
- Short talks from community members
- Integration and reflection practices

### [Key Themes and Insights](/gatherings/cscsc25/key-themes/)

**Foundations Workshop** explored the territory through discussions on:

- Complexity Science fundamentals and applications
- Contemplative Studies research overview and philosophy
- Complexity leadership and wise action in uncertainty
- Polarity in nature and Goethean phenomenology
- Dynamical systems science of mindfulness and meditation
- Culture as emergent order

**Methods Workshop** aligned on objectives and approaches through:

- Contemplative methods and philosophical frameworks
- Methodological challenges in bridging first-person and third-person inquiry
- The role of resonant, felt sense, and truth in scientific inquiry
- Bayesian approaches to exploration, motivation, and pleasure
- Empirical and quantitative methods for studying complex systems in mindfulness data

**Core Questions Workshop** used Open Space Technology format for consensus-building on the field's fundamental inquiries and future directions.
