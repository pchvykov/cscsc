## Remember the last time your work felt alive?

Not productive — *alive*.

Here's the thing nobody talks about: we learn early that the work isn't supposed to feel good *now*. It's supposed to pay off *later*. So we tolerate. "For the greater good." We work for the result — the publication, the launch, the recognition. And meanwhile the actual hours of our actual lives pass in a kind of half-sleep — not feeling our bodies because we don't want to acknowledge our suffering. "Soon it will all be worth it." 

<aside class="callout bg-purple">
<span class="icon" aria-hidden="true">⚡</span>
<div class="body">

We keep waiting for *something* that never quite arrives. What if that something could be *now*, right in the middle of your work?

</div>
</aside>

The resistance you feel opening a document is the same resistance a meditator feels sitting with discomfort. And it responds to the same thing: honest, gentle attention. When you bring that quality to your actual work — surrounded by people doing the same — something shifts. 

<span class="bg-teal">>> Your work</span> <span class="bg-teal">*becomes*</span> <span class="bg-teal">your true aliveness &lt;&lt;</span>

> *This is a procrastination vaccine: participants finally get to the projects they'd been avoiding for years, or get clarity on what they need to do to make their dream, and start doing it!*

## What the week looks like

You bring your project — the paper, the code, the book — whatever's alive for you. We work together in focused blocks, cook together, play together, and get honest with each other about what we actually care about — and what we've been avoiding.

<aside class="callout bg-orange">
<span class="icon" aria-hidden="true">🔥</span>
<div class="body">

**Days 1–2: Deep dive**

90-minute work blocks, no guilt, no rules. Like a hackathon without the performance anxiety. Between blocks, quick mindfulness practice or check-ins with a buddy: *"What are you noticing?"*

</div>
</aside>

<aside class="callout bg-yellow">
<span class="icon" aria-hidden="true">🔍</span>
<div class="body">

**Day 3: Patterns emerge**

Some blocks felt like flow, some felt like pulling teeth. We look at that contrast together — not to fix it, but to see what it's telling you. 

</div>
</aside>

<aside class="callout bg-purple">
<span class="icon" aria-hidden="true">🪞</span>
<div class="body">

**Days 3–4: It gets real**

Small group conversations start weaving in — no advice, no fixing, just being seen. You might notice you're clenching your jaw before you open a document. You might catch yourself performing "deep work" for an audience that isn't there. You might realize the project you're avoiding isn't boring — it's terrifying because it actually matters. Work becomes a mirror for everything else.

</div>
</aside>

<aside class="callout bg-teal">
<span class="icon" aria-hidden="true">🌄</span>
<div class="body">

**Day 5: Landing**

A final work block. A closing circle. Reflection. Not *"what should you change?"* — more like *"what are you already doing, now that you're listening?"*

</div>
</aside>

> Throughout: morning movement, communal cooking, fires, games, stargazing. The social warmth is what makes the transformation land.

## Who this is for

Researchers, writers, developers, designers, creative and intellectual workers — anyone who chose work they love and suspects they might have lost touch with the joy of it.

You might recognize yourself here:

- You do meaningful work, but are waiting for the menial tasks to pay off — tolerating now for the sake of later
- You work all the time but rarely feel present in it — more performing than creating
- You want to spend a week with people who get it — no small talk, no performing, no pretending everything's fine

Some contemplative experience helps but isn't required. What matters is honesty.

*Not for you if you're looking for a productivity system or a quick fix to avoid facing your real self.*

<details class="toggle">
<summary><h3>Why this matters beyond you</h3></summary>
<div class="body">

75% of young Europeans say the future is frightening. Suicide is the leading cause of death for 15–29 year-olds in Europe. Our parents and grandparents poured their lives into building a more comfortable world for us — and somehow created a generation that is more miserable than any before.

This isn't a policy failure. It's a *transmission* failure. When we do our work in a state of stress and self-sacrifice — tolerating, enduring, waiting for the payoff — the things we build carry that energy. We might create comfortable systems, but they feel hollow and dead inside.

<aside class="callout bg-red">
<span class="icon" aria-hidden="true">🌍</span>
<div class="body">

The world doesn't need more output from disconnected people. It needs people who are *alive* in their work — so that what they create actually enlivens the people who receive it.

</div>
</aside>

This retreat is a small experiment in that direction.

<summary><h3>About the facilitator</h3></summary>
<div class="body">

**Pavel Chvykov** — [pchvykov.com](http://pchvykov.com)

<figure class="narrow"><img src="/media/31cd8cae0cfed1217a.webp" alt="" loading="lazy" /></figure>

PhD in physics from MIT. Published in *Science*. Built a research career in complexity science and origins of life. For years I kept telling myself that I am doing this for the greater good. That the next paper, the next position would finally make it feel worth it. And sometimes it did! But I kept sacrificing the lived experience of my days on the altar of abstract knowledge and results — tolerating now for the promise of later.

The real shift didn't come from a better system or a bigger grant. It came from tantra, authentic relating, and contemplative practice — from learning to feel what's actually happening while I work, instead of living for the output. I stopped looking for the perfect system and started running these retreats instead — because I haven't figured this out, and doing it together is the best thing that seems to help.

I'll be doing my own work alongside you, having my own struggles, naming my own patterns. The modeling is the teaching.
