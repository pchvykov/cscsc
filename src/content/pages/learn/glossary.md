## Philosophy

<dl class="terms">
<div><dt>Reason <small>(S. T. Coleridge)</small></dt><dd>The one power that generates all reality, both in nature and in thought, by separating and detaching in order to reunite.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Understanding <small>(S. T. Coleridge)</small></dt><dd>The power of generalizing from data and associating stable names to sense impressions.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Imagination <small>(S. T. Coleridge)</small></dt><dd>The power that crystallizes the ideas of reason into categories of understanding.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Idea <small>(S. T. Coleridge)</small></dt><dd>A constituent "part" of reason, expressed as law in nature and as concept in the understanding.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Spirituality</dt><dd>awareness of the ineffable content of experience and practices for its cultivation</dd></div>
<div><dt>Material ontology (ontological realism)</dt><dd>A view that reality is fundamentally made of discrete, independent <em>things</em> or substances (e.g., atoms, objects) with inherent properties.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Process ontology</dt><dd>A view that reality is fundamentally made of unfolding <em>processes</em>, events, and becoming rather than static things. Being is a verb.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Relational ontology</dt><dd>A view that reality is fundamentally made of the <em>relationships</em> and interactions between things. Entities are defined by their connections.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Post-structuralism</dt><dd>A philosophical movement skeptical of stable meanings, objective truth, and fixed structures, emphasizing instead how knowledge is shaped by language, power, and context.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Being</dt><dd>The state or essence of existing; the fundamental nature of reality and existence itself.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Subject</dt><dd>The conscious self or mind that perceives, experiences, and acts upon the world.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Object</dt><dd>A thing or phenomenon that is perceived, experienced, or acted upon by a subject.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Form</dt><dd>The essential structure, pattern, or organizing principle of a thing that makes it what it is.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Nature</dt><dd>1. The inherent character or essence of something. 2. The physical universe and its phenomena as a whole, operating according to discoverable laws.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Worldview</dt><dd>The deep, often implicit assumptions about what is real, what counts as evidence and what matters, which shape research before it starts. They show up in what questions get asked, what models get built and what counts as an explanation.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Polarity</dt><dd>The presence of two opposite, contrasting, yet complementary and interdependent principles (e.g., subject/object, order/disorder, yin/yang). In our practice, a tension held as generative rather than resolved (e.g. problem-solving vs contemplative intelligence; wisdom vs knowledge).</dd></div>
<div><dt>Eudaimonia <small>(Aristotle)</small></dt><dd>Often translated as "human flourishing." The state of living in accordance with one's true nature and potential, achieving a life of meaning and virtue. A goal shared by contemplative and complexity-informed practices.</dd></div>
</dl>

## Science &amp; Inquiry

<dl class="terms">
<div><dt>Observer-dependence</dt><dd>The degree to which observing or modeling a system changes the system. It runs on a continuum: the physicist in the lab (observer-independent), the economist whose models move the market (weak dependence), the meditator studying their own mind (observation <em>is</em> the system changing).</dd></div>
<div><dt>Second-order cybernetics <small>(Heinz von Foerster)</small></dt><dd>The study of observing systems rather than observed ones: cybernetics that includes the observer as part of the system it describes.</dd></div>
<div><dt>First-, second- and third-person inquiry</dt><dd>Three modes of knowing that we try to hold together: first-person (direct investigation of one's own experience, e.g. contemplative practice), second-person (dialogue and relational exchange that surfaces hidden assumptions), and third-person (formal models, data, reproducible findings).</dd></div>
<div><dt>Honest science</dt><dd>Doing research from the questions and worldview that actually drive you, not the fundable or performed version. Includes examining how incentives and unexamined assumptions have shaped your work.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Epistemic debugging</dt><dd>Noticing where fear, ego, and proxy metrics (Goodhart effects) have distorted your scientific judgment, as a first step to correcting it.</dd></div>
</dl>

## Contemplative Studies

<dl class="terms">
<div><dt>Contemplation</dt><dd>A practice of sustained, focused attention and open awareness, often aimed at deepening insight into the nature of reality, self, or the divine.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Mindfulness</dt><dd>The practice of paying deliberate, non-judgmental attention to present-moment experience.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Ritual</dt><dd>A formalized, patterned sequence of symbolic activities, often repeated, that connects individuals to community, tradition, and meaning. Unlike argument, ritual can change values and goals, so every institution (academia included) is a ritual system, designed or not.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Ceremony</dt><dd>A more formal, structured event or occasion that incorporates rituals to mark a significant transition, celebration, or milestone</dd></div>
<div><dt>Ecology of practices</dt><dd>a framework for understanding how a collection of interconnected and interdependent practices work together to foster personal and collective growth, wisdom, and meaning</dd></div>
<div><dt>Prayer</dt><dd>A contemplative practice of communion or communication, often addressed to a divine or transcendent reality, involving gratitude, supplication, or silent presence.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Yoga <small>(Lit. "union")</small></dt><dd>A diverse set of physical, mental, and spiritual disciplines originating in India aimed at stilling the mind and realizing a unified state of being.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Conscious communication practices</dt><dd>Dialogical practices (e.g., Nonviolent Communication, circling) that emphasize deep listening, presence, and authentic expression to foster mutual understanding.</dd></div>
<div><dt>God</dt><dd>The ultimate, transcendent reality; the ground of all being; a supreme object of worship and devotion. Definitions vary immensely across traditions.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Logos <small>(Lit. "word," "reason," "principle")</small></dt><dd>A concept representing the underlying ordering principle of the cosmos, divine reason, or the structuring intelligence behind reality.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Spirit</dt><dd>The non-material animating principle of life, consciousness, or divine presence; often contrasted with matter or form.</dd></div>
<div><dt>‘gods’ / deities</dt><dd>Ritually animated, symbolic attractors within a consensus reality that coordinate shared meaning, values, and social cohesion.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Interbeing <small>(Thich Nhat Hanh)</small></dt><dd>A Buddhist-inspired term expressing profound interdependence and relationality: "to be is to inter-be." A perfect contemplative correlate to relational ontology and networks.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Wisdom</dt><dd>A capacity, not a fixed content: to perceive clearly, respond appropriately and hold complexity, including knowing which kind of knowledge fits which context. It grows through practice over time and draws on first-, second- and third-person perspectives.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Research monastery</dt><dd>A community organized around a shared practice of inquiry, where contemplative formation and rigorous investigation are integrated rather than run as parallel tracks. Its purpose is to hold and pass on knowledge that does not travel easily as papers.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Restitution</dt><dd>Structured, skilled, collective sense-making after a shared experience. Here it is treated as the step where transformation actually happens, not as an add-on to the experience.</dd></div>
</dl>

### Religion

<dl class="terms">
<div><dt>Religion</dt><dd>A system of beliefs, practices, rituals, and ethics centered on a relationship with the sacred, transcendent, or ultimate reality, often organized within a community.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Faith</dt><dd>Trust, confidence, or allegiance in something not immediately susceptible to rigorous proof (e.g., a doctrine, a person, a transcendent reality).</dd></div>
<div><dt>Tradition</dt><dd>The transmission of customs, beliefs, and practices from generation to generation, providing continuity and a shared framework of meaning.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Dogma</dt><dd>Static, authoritative constructs that anchor a community's shared beliefs and identity, enforcing doctrinal cohesion.</dd></div>
</dl>

## Complexity Science

<dl class="terms">
<div><dt>Complexity</dt><dd>The study of systems characterized by a large number of interacting components whose collective behavior is <em>emergent</em> and cannot be easily predicted from the properties of the individual parts.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Science</dt><dd>A systematic enterprise that builds and organizes knowledge in the form of testable explanations and predictions about the universe.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Law</dt><dd>A scientific principle that describes a consistent, observable pattern in nature under specific conditions (e.g., law of gravity).</dd></div>
<div><dt>Disorder</dt><dd>A measure of randomness or unpredictability in a system; often quantified as <strong><em>entropy</em></strong>.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Emergence</dt><dd>The process where complex patterns and behaviors <em>arise</em> from the interactions of many simple components, without any central control. The whole is greater than the sum of its parts.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Self-organization</dt><dd>The spontaneous emergence of order and structure in a system from local interactions between its components, without external direction.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Non-linearity</dt><dd>A relationship where cause and effect are not proportional. Small changes can have large effects (butterfly effect), and large changes can have small effects.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Feedback loops</dt><dd>A process where the output of a system is circled back as input, influencing subsequent outputs. <em>Positive</em> feedback amplifies change; <em>negative</em> feedback dampens it and promotes stability.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Edge of chaos / criticality</dt><dd>A transition zone between order and disorder where a system is maximally adaptable, complex, and capable of computation and emergence.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Self-organized criticality</dt><dd>A property of systems that naturally evolve toward a critical state (the edge of chaos) where small perturbations can trigger events of any size (e.g., avalanches in a sandpile).</dd></div>
<div><dt>Phase transition</dt><dd>A radical, qualitative change in the state or structure of a system when a control parameter crosses a critical threshold (e.g., water freezing or boiling).</dd></div>
<div><dt>Universality</dt><dd>The observation that vastly different complex systems (e.g., magnets, forests, financial markets) can exhibit identical critical behavior and scaling laws, suggesting deep common principles.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Scale-free behavior</dt><dd>A pattern where the statistical distribution of events (e.g., size of avalanches, city populations, node connections in a network) follows a power law, meaning there is no characteristic "typical" scale.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Causal chains</dt><dd>A linear sequence of events where one cause leads to an effect, which becomes the cause of another effect.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Counter-factual causality</dt><dd>A form of causation defined not by a direct chain of events, but by the <em>difference</em> that an intervention makes. "If X had not happened, Y would not have occurred."</dd></div>
<div><dt>Effects of first-order, second-order, higher-order</dt><dd>The direct and indirect consequences of an action. A <em>first-order</em> effect is the immediate result. A <em>second-order</em> effect is the consequence of the consequence, and so on.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Goodhart’s law</dt><dd>"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure." An indicator loses its value when it is used for control.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Typicality</dt><dd>A property where a single, random instance of a complex system is highly representative of the entire class of such systems, allowing a random model to accurately predict the behavior of a specific, real-world case.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Autopoiesis <small>(Lit. "self-making")</small></dt><dd>The property of a system that continuously regenerates itself and defines its own boundary. A key bridge between biology (Maturana & Varela) and systems thinking.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Resilience</dt><dd>The capacity of a system to absorb disturbance and reorganize while undergoing change so as to retain essentially the same function, structure, and identity.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Sympoiesis <small>(Lit. "making-with")</small></dt><dd>A concept contrasting autopoiesis; it describes collectively-producing, boundary-less systems (e.g., ecosystems, coral reefs) that do not have self-defined boundaries. Highly relevant for relational ontology.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Semantic Information</dt><dd>A measure of the <em>causal, functional, or strategic value</em> of information or a state for a system in maintaining its existence (e.g., predicting its environment, making a decision).</dd></div>
<div><dt>A-life (Artificial Life)</dt><dd>The interdisciplinary study of life and life-like <em>processes</em> (e.g., evolution, adaptation, self-organization) by simulating them in computers, robotics, or biochemistry. Its goal is not just to model life-as-we-know-it but to understand the general principles of <em>life-as-it-could-be</em>.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Telics <small>(Matthieu Barbier)</small></dt><dd>A proposed formal language for goals, roles and purpose: explaining a part by what it does for a larger whole, not by what it is made of. It is meant to do for purpose what reductionism did for mechanism.</dd></div>
</dl>

### Game Theory

<dl class="terms">
<div><dt>Game theory</dt><dd>The study of mathematical models of strategic interaction among rational decision-makers.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Nash equilibrium</dt><dd>A solution concept where no player can benefit by unilaterally changing their strategy while the other players keep theirs unchanged.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Prisoner’s dilemma</dt><dd>A classic game that shows why two rational individuals might not cooperate, even if it appears to be in their best interest to do so.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Pareto optimality</dt><dd>A state where no individual can be made better off without making at least one other individual worse off.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Problem of cooperation</dt><dd>The challenge of explaining how cooperation evolves in a world of self-interested agents, where defection is often individually beneficial.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Problem of coordination</dt><dd>The challenge of how vast numbers of individuals, without central direction, converge on shared conventions (e.g., language, traffic laws) to create a single, functional societal system.</dd></div>
</dl>

### Cognitive Science

<dl class="terms">
<div><dt>Cognitive science</dt><dd>The interdisciplinary study of the mind and intelligence, embracing philosophy, psychology, AI, neuroscience, and linguistics.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Bayesian inference</dt><dd>A method of probability-based reasoning where prior beliefs are updated with new evidence to form posterior beliefs.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Bayes brain / Predictive processing <small>(also predictive coding)</small></dt><dd>A theory that the brain is a probabilistic inference engine that constantly generates and updates models of the world to predict sensory input. In predictive coding, a specific version, the brain minimizes "prediction errors" (the difference between its predictions and sensory data) by either updating its model or acting on the world.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Active inference</dt><dd>The process of minimizing prediction error by selectively sampling the world through action (e.g., moving to get a better view).</dd></div>
<div><dt>Free energy principle</dt><dd>A unifying theory that proposes all self-organizing systems (including brains) act to minimize "free energy," a measure of surprise or prediction error, to maintain their order.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Agency</dt><dd>The capacity of a system to pursue goals and act on its environment and its own states. In our use it comes in degrees, from cells and tissues to people and collectives, and is treated as a lens whose usefulness is judged by what it lets us predict and do.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Enaction</dt><dd>The view that cognition is not the representation of a pre-given world but is <em>brought forth</em> through a history of embodied action within an environment.</dd></div>
<div><dt>4E Cognition</dt><dd>A framework grouping theories of cognition as <strong>E</strong>mbodied, <strong>E</strong>mbedded, <strong>E</strong>nactive, and <strong>E</strong>xtended. Directly links cognitive science to systems thinking.</dd></div>
</dl>

### Sociology

<dl class="terms">
<div><dt>Sociology</dt><dd>The study of the development, structure, and functioning of human society.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Network</dt><dd>A structure composed of <em>nodes</em> (e.g., people, neurons) connected by <em>links</em> (e.g., friendships, synapses), defining how information, influence, or resources flow.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Opinion dynamics</dt><dd>The study of how individuals' opinions, beliefs, and attitudes change through social interaction within a network.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Memetics</dt><dd>The theoretical study of <strong><em>memes</em></strong>: units of cultural information (e.g., ideas, styles, practices) that spread from person to person through imitation (like <em>genes</em> in biology).</dd></div>
<div><dt>Memetic evolution</dt><dd>The process by which memes vary, are selected, and are transmitted, leading to the evolution of culture, analogous to biological evolution.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Social (collective) imaginaries</dt><dd>The shared, often unspoken pictures of the world that organize how a society lives. Like a target form in development, they act as attractors that pull social reality into being, and they can be sensed, questioned and re-formed.</dd></div>
<div><dt>The Social Imaginary (Castoriadis)</dt><dd>the fundamental capacity for a society to create itself, its own institutions, and its own world.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Consensus construct <small>(also consensus reality)</small></dt><dd>A reality that exists and stays stable because many observers or participants keep agreeing on it: social cases like money, borders and institutions, and, on a broader reading, physical objectivity itself (quantum Darwinism) and the results of the scientific method.</dd></div>
</dl>
