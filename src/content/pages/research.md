#### **Agency & Intelligence as Causes**

Moving beyond mechanistic views of life to understand organisms as goal-directed, intelligent agents challenges reductionist frameworks and opens space for contemplative perspectives on consciousness, purpose, and the nature of mind across scales—from cells to ecosystems to AI.

[Read on Noema](https://www.noemamag.com/ai-could-be-a-bridge-toward-diverse-intelligence/)

#### **Do We Have the Right Kind of Math for Roles, Goals and Meaning?**

Arguing that we lack a rigorous scientific method for understanding how contexts and purposes arise from larger contexts, and how they select on subsystems—a "telic science" that would be to purpose what reductionism is to mechanism. Existing formalisms (optimization, game theory, dynamics) answer the wrong type of questions, serving post hoc rather than capturing the essence of function and meaning.

*Matthieu Barbier*

[Read on LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/KhzQMcw4ard9LxaxL/do-we-have-the-right-kind-of-math-for-roles-goals-and)

#### **Agent Above, Atom Below: How Agents Causally Emerge from Their Underlying Microphysics**

Exploring how genuine agency can causally emerge from underlying physical processes, bridging the gap between microphysical descriptions and agent-level explanations—showing that agents are not merely reducible to their constituent atoms but possess real causal powers at their own level of organization.

[Read on Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-75726-1_6)

#### **Glossary: Novel Terms for Biological Agency**

Defining novel terms—such as "agential material," "anatomical compiler," "axis of persuadability," and "bioprompting"—to frame biological systems as possessing varying degrees of autonomy, problem-solving, and capacities for communication and control, extending traditional language to encompass the agency and competencies found in cells, tissues, and engineered constructs.

*Michael Levin*

[Read on Levin’s site](https://drmichaellevin.org/resources/#question13) 

#### **Memory, Cognition, and Agency as Analytical Lenses**

Concepts like memory, cognition, and agency are described as analytical lenses—metaphors, observer-dependent and context-relative—that should be defined pragmatically, based on their ability to enhance scientific prediction, control, and discovery, rather than as absolute or purely philosophical categories.

*Michael Levin*

[Read on Levin’s site](https://drmichaellevin.org/resources/#question14)

#### Project proposal: Ecological Complexity and Individuality

This proposal bridges modern ecological modeling and contemplative inquiry of the “self” to address two interrelated gaps: (1) the reductionist view of individuality based on outdated neo-Darwinian frameworks and (2) the inadequacy of current conservation policies to balance competing priorities. First, we use complexity science—by analyzing cooperation, symbiosis, and ecosystem-level dynamics—to critique atomistic notions of individuality prevalent in the modern West, proposing instead a model of identity rooted in systemic interdependence in one’s social contexts. This supports revised ethical frameworks for human agency and community that align with modern understanding of ecology. Second, we translate contemplative traditions’ structured methods for perceiving interconnectedness (e.g., multi-level symbolic systems akin to Aristotle’s “four causes”) into tools for conservation science. These methods will clarify how to prioritize conservation targets (species, ecosystems, functions) by resolving tensions between competing values like biodiversity, ecosystem services and wilderness integrity that currently plague conservation efforts.

#### **Social Reality as Collective Imaginaries**

Just as embryogenesis is shaped by target morphologies—where cells organize toward an envisioned form—societies are shaped by their collective beliefs, rituals, and social imaginaries. These shared visions of what ought to be serve as attractors that pull social reality into being, making the imagined manifest. This perspective challenges mechanistic social network models by recognizing ritual and collective intentionality as fundamental technologies for coordinating behavior, transmitting values, and literally constructing the social world we inhabit.

#### Project proposal: Money as Collective Yajna

In Hindu thought, reality is constructed at three levels (Bhavana): individual perception, relational agreement, and collective consensus, with each level maintained by its corresponding rituals. Yajna is a core fire-sacrifice ritual used to maintain collective realities, such as ethical or religious truths. Applying this lens to money, we propose that its stability depends on its maintenance at all three levels: its ability to simultaneously function as a personal belief (trust in<br>its value), an interpersonal agreement (medium of exchange), and a collective institution (legal and cultural enforcement). By modeling these layers as interdependent networks of rituals, we aim to explore how money transitions from fragile subjective trust to robust collective reality. Integrating case-studies with agent-based modeling, this framework should help to formalize why money destabilizes under conditions like hyperinflation or loss of institutional trust. More broadly, this may pave the way towards a ritual-focused modeling paradigm for social realities.

#### Project proposal: Towards a theory of mindfulness

Develop a computational theory of mindfulness and related practices by integrating the Bayesian brain hypothesis, predictive coding, neural annealing, and spin-glass models. There is some work framing mindfulness as a process of modulating prediction errors and priors, while the recently proposed neural annealing framework emphasizes high-energy behavioral activation for reorganizing neural patterns. We suggest viewing rituals, mantras, and prayer as encoding culturally evolved priors that guide this reorganization. We propose modeling these dynamics using Boltzmann machine-like systems, where rituals act as externally applied field patterns, along with structured annealing schedules to stabilize desirable cognitive states. By integrating neuroscience, machine learning, and cultural studies, this framework builds towards a unified model for understanding and optimizing contemplative practices.
